From Help Desk to IT Assistant: What I Learned

My first real role in IT was as a Help Desk associate, and now I work as an IT Assistant at COMPASS Training Center. Both jobs taught me that support is about so much more than fixing computers. Here are the biggest lessons.

1. Troubleshooting Is a Process, Not Magic

The first question is always the same: healthy or broken? I learned to work top-down — check cables and power, check the obvious settings, then dig deeper. Write everything you try down; it keeps you from repeating steps and helps you spot patterns across tickets.

  1. Gather information from the user first
  2. Test the most likely cause first
  3. Document every step and the outcome
  4. Communicate the fix back in plain language

2. Patience and Communication Are Half the Job

Users describe issues in their own words, not network terms. A "my internet is slow" report can hide dozens of root causes. I learned to ask clear questions, explain what I'm doing, and always close the loop with the end user so they know the issue is resolved.

3. Documentation Is a Superpower

Repeating tickets are the best signal that something needs documentation. Over time I built quick reference notes for common fixes — printer issues, login problems, connectivity checks. Ten minutes of writing saves an hour of re-diagnosing later.

4. Every Day Teaches You Something New

From setup and deployment to backups, network configs, and user management, support roles touch everything. That breadth is exactly what helped me grow from Help Desk into an IT Assistant — and what keeps pushing me toward cloud, networking, and development.

Support roles are the best foundation for a career in IT. You touch every layer of the stack and learn how real systems actually behave.

Tips for Fresh Grads

Say yes to the messy tickets, keep a learning log, and be honest when you're unsure — then go find the answer. Curiosity compounds. Two years in, I can trace problems that used to leave me stuck, and I'm still just getting started.
